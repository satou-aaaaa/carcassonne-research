Where a strong AI places its pieces

We had this project's strongest AI (gen1144, adopted 2026-11-11; the same AI as "Strongest" on the play page) play 48 games against itself and recorded every move. For comparison we used "an AI that just picks the move scoring the most points this turn" (the greedy AI below). The strong AI chose the same move as the greedy AI only 31% of the time. The other 69% of moves are full of tips a beginner can copy.

MeeplesHow to use your 7

You only have 7 meeples. This is where the strong AI differed most.

Don't use them all up early

It's tempting to place a meeple whenever you can, but the strong AI declines to place one more often than the greedy AI and uses them more slowly. At the end of the opening (48 tiles left) it still has 1.5 in hand on average. One spare meeple lets you seize big chances, such as joining an opponent's city or claiming a large field. From the middle game on, the strong AI also uses almost all of them (0.6 on average with 24 tiles left), so think "don't rush to place them all" rather than "hoard them".

Only claim roads that will end soon

Roads are cheap at 1 point per tile, and they never finish if the ends aren't fixed. The strong AI mostly puts meeples on roads when extending an existing road, and those meeples come back quickly. Only 19% of its road meeples started a new road.

Start your own new cities and grow them

44% of the strong AI's meeples went into cities (greedy AI: 35%). And 63% of those were placed on the first tile of a new, unclaimed city. A completed city is worth a lot, 2 points per tile plus 2 per shield, and a city of your own is hard for others to interfere with.

Few farmers, in fields with many completed cities

Farmers (meeples in fields) stay until the end, but at 9.9 points each on average they earn more than any other placement. Even so, the strong AI places only 2.4 farmers per game on average, fewer than the greedy AI (3.3). It spreads them out, 0.9, 0.8 and 0.7 on average in the opening, middle and end game, and late on it puts returning meeples into fields that touch many completed cities.

With farmers, location matters more than count. The average gap in field points between winners and losers is +7, and it grows or shrinks with the AI version and the luck of the games (where the gap comes from). Don't place so many early that you run short for cities, but when you find a field where completed cities are likely to gather, claim it.

TilesWhere to place them

The strong AI doesn't just extend its own features; it also gets involved with its opponent's. It placed a tile touching an opponent's feature on 16% of turns, versus 11% for the greedy AI.

Join your opponent's big city and share it

You can't place directly in a city your opponent occupies, but you can start your own small city next to it with a meeple and connect them later with a tile. With equal meeples, both players score in full. In the strong AI's games, 11% of all points came from such shared features.

Make your opponent's city uncompletableAdvanced

If you leave a hole next to a city that none of the remaining tiles can fill, the city can never be completed. Instead of 2 points per tile when completed, it scores only 1 per tile at the end. The strong AI played this move 5.9 times as often as the greedy AI.

The strong AI plays this 0.90 times per player per game on average (86 times in 48 games). But you can only aim for it if you count the remaining tiles. As a beginner, focus on completing your own cities first; "when unsure where to place, consider spots that get in the way of your opponent's city" is enough.

Fill around your monastery yourself and go for 9

A monastery meeple scores 8.3 points on average, but 51% are never completed. When there are gaps around its monastery, the strong AI fills them with its own tiles.

ScoringWhere the gap comes from

Winners pile up points in cities

Comparing winners and losers by source of points, winners also scored more in cities. From the winner's side, the gaps are cities +9, fields +7, monasteries −1 and roads +2. Apart from cities, the gaps change order or even flip with the AI version and the luck of the games, so wins come from many small gains rather than one decisive factor. Field points also come from completed cities, so cities are the foundation. When in doubt, prefer moves that complete your own cities and that claim fields touching completed cities.

Summary

  1. Don't use up your meeples early. Having 1–2 left at the end of the opening is about right.
  2. Only claim roads that look like they'll end soon.
  3. Start your own new cities and grow them. Winners score more in cities (field points also come from completed cities).
  4. Keep farmers few. Late in the game, put spare meeples in fields that touch many completed cities.
  5. Join your opponent's big cities and share them. Once you're comfortable, also look for moves that make them uncompletable.
  6. Fill in around your monastery yourself to get the meeple back quickly.

Now put it into practice. Try the next-move quiz to see if you pick the same moves as the AI, and when you play the AI, use the hints and focus on one of these tips per game.

About the numbers on this page

  • The strong AI is the same AI as "Strongest" on the play page (gen1144, adopted 2026-11-11: a learned evaluation function + 12,000 Monte Carlo tree search simulations + 10-move lookahead + a final check of the top candidates against the opponent's best reply). Each time the AI gets stronger, we replay the games and rebuild the numbers and diagrams on this page. It may have habits that differ from how strong human players play.
  • With only 48 strong-AI games, differences of a few percent are within the margin of error. This page covers only trends with large differences.
  • Rules: base game, two players, farmers score 3 points per completed city (3rd edition).
  • To reproduce: record with scripts/record_habits.py, aggregate with scripts/summarize_habits.py, and build this page with scripts/pick_examples.py and scripts/build_tips_page.py.
