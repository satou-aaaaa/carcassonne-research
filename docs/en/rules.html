Carcassonne · Base game rules · 2–5 players

Your first game of Carcassonne

Players add tiles one at a time to grow a shared map, and score with the pieces (meeples) they place on the cities and roads they build. This page explains, with diagrams, all the rules you need to play.

1What you do on your turn

  1. Draw a tile and place it. Put it next to a tile already on the table so that the edges match.
  2. You may place a meeple. You can put one of your meeples on one city, road, monastery or field on the tile you just placed. You don't have to.
  3. Score what is completed. When a city, road or monastery is completed, the player with meeples on it scores, and the meeples go back to their owners.

The game ends when the tiles run out. Then you score unfinished features and fields, and the player with the most points wins.

2Placing tiles

If you draw a tile that fits nowhere, put it back in the box unused and draw another.

3Placing meeples

  • Each player has 7 meeples. You can only place one on the tile you just placed.
  • If a connected city or road already has a meeple on it, you can't place there (even if it is your own).
  • Two separately claimed cities may later join, leaving several meeples in one city. Then the player with the most meeples scores; if tied, all tied players score in full.
  • A meeple stays put until its feature is completed. Meeples in fields (farmers) never come back before the end.

5End of the game

The game ends once the last tile is placed and scored. For each meeple still on the board, add the "at the end" points from the table above. The player with the highest total wins.

6Tips for beginners

  • Building small cities and closing them is the surest way to score at first.
  • You only have 7 meeples. Put one on a long road that is slow to finish and it won't come back for a while.
  • When in doubt, look for something you can complete right now. You score, and your meeple comes back.

About the rules on this page

These are the current rules of the base game (72 tiles). Farmers score 3 points per completed city (older editions counted differently). The diagrams are real positions played with this project's rules engine, and the points in the text are checked against the engine's scoring (scripts/build_rules_page.py). The examples assume two players, but 2–5 players use the same rules.
